This post provides an introduction to the terminology of options trading.

Calls and Puts

  • Call Option. A call option is a right to buy or take a long position in a given asset (typically a security, commodity, index or futures contract) at a fixed price at a specified future date.
  • Put Option. : A put option is a right to sell or take a short position in a given asset at fixed price at a specified future date.

Differences between futures and options

Futures Contract

  • A futures contract requires delivery at a fixed price.
  • The buyer and seller of a future contract both have obligations which they must meet.
  • The seller must make delivery and the buyer must take delivery of the asset.

Options

  • The buyer of an option, however, has a choice.

  • He or she can choose to take delivery(a call) or make delivery(a put).

  • If a buyer of an option choose to either make or take delivery, the seller of the option is obligated to take the other side.

  • In option trading all rights lie with the buyer and all obligations with the seller.

  • The asset to be bought or sold under the terms of the option is the underlying asset or , more simply, the underlying.

  • The exercise price or strike price , is the price at which the underlying will be delivered should the holder of an option choose to exercise his right to buy or sell

  • The date after which the option may no longer be exercise is the expiration date.

  • Once an option is exercised, it ceases to exist, just as if it has been allowed to expire unexercised.

  • In addition to its underlying contract, exercise price, expiration date and type, an option is further identified by the conditions of exercise. An option is American whereby the holder can exercise the option at any time prior to expiration or European whereby the holder can exercise the option only on expiration day. All U.S exchange traded stock and futures options are American.

Example :

Exchange traded option : The buyer of a crude oil October 21 calls on the New York Mercantile Exchange has the right to take a long positio in one October crude oil futures contract for 1000 barrels of crude oil ( the underlying), as a price $21 per barrel ( the exercise price), on or before October expiration ( the expiration date). Chicago Board Options Exchage : The buyer of a General Electric March 80 put on the Chicago Board Options Exchange has the right to take a short position in 100 shares of General Electic stock ( the underlying) at a price of $80 per share ( the exercise price), on or before March Expiration ( the expiration date).

Exercise And Assignment of Options

A trader who owns a call or a put option has the right to exercise that option prior to its expiration date, thereby converting the option into a long underlying position, in the case of a call, or a short underlying position in case of a put.

  • A trader who exercises a crude oil October 21 call has chosen to take a long position in one October crude oil futures contract at $21 per barrel.
  • A trader who exercises a GE March 80 put has chosen to take a short position in 100 shares of GE stock at $80 per share.

Once an option is exercised it ceases to exist.

As in any competitive market, an option’s price or premium , is determined by supply and demand. Buyers and sellers make competitive bids and offers in the marketplace. When a bit and offer coincide, a trade is make.

The premium paid for an option can be seperated into two components : The intrinsic value and the time value.

An options intrinsic value is the amount which would be credited to the option holder’s account if he were to exercise the option and close out the position against the underlying contract at the current market price.

Example : With gold trading at $435 per ounce, the intrinsic value of a $400 call is $35. By exercising his option, the holder of the $400 call can buy gold at $400 per ounce. If he sells gold at market price of $435 per ounce, $35 per ounce will be credited to his account. With a certain stock trading at $62, the intrinsic value of a $70 put is $8. By exercising this option, the holder of the put can sell the stock at $70 per share. If he then buys the stock back at the market price of $62 , he will show a total credit of $8.

A call with only have intrinsic value if its exercise price is less than the current market price of the underlying contract A put will only have intrinsic value if its exercise price is greater than the current market price of the underlying contract.

The amount of intrinsic value if the amount by which the call’s exercise price is less than the current underlying price, or the amoung by which the put’s exercise price is greater than the current underlying price. No option can have an intrinsic value less than zero.

The additional amount of premium beyond the intrinsic value which traders are willing to pay for an option is the time value, sometimes also referred to as the option’s time premium or extrinsic value.

An option’s premium is always composed of precisely its intrinsic value and its time value. If a $400 gold call is tradding at $50 with gold at $435 per ounce, the time value of the call must be $15, since the intrinsic value is $35.
The two components must add up to the option’s total premium of $50.

Even though an option’s premium is always composed of its intrinsic value and its time value, it is possible for one or both of these components to be zero. If the option had no intrinsic value, its price in the marketplace will consist solely of time value. If the option has no time value, its price will consist of solely intrinsic value.

Any option which as a positive intrinsic value is said to be in-the-money by the amount of the intrinsic value. With a stock at $44, a $40 call is $4 in-the-money. An option which has no intrinsic value is said to be out-of-the-money. The price of an out-of-the-money option consist solely of time value.

In order to be in-the-money, a call (put) must have an exercise price lower ( higher) than the current price of the underlying contract. Note that if a call is in-the-money, a put wit hthe same exercise price and udnerlying contract must be out-of-the-money.

An option whose exercise price is identical to the curent price of the underlying contract is said to be at-the-money.

Market Integrity

In order to gurantee that the integrity of the market will be maintained, each option exchange has establish a progression of responsibility for the fulfillment of the terms of an option.

  • The primary responsibility falls to the individual trader. If the seller of an option is assigned, he must be prepared to take the required long or short position in the underlying contract at the specified exercise price.
  • If an individual trader is unable to fulfill the terms of the contract, the responsibility falls to the trader’s clearing firm. A clearing firm is a member firm of the exhcnage which processer trades make by individual, and which agrees to fulfill any financial obligation arising from those trades.
  • If the clearning firm cannot fulfull the terms of the contract, the final responsibility rests with the Clearing house.Each exchange has established or has become a member of, a clearning house which gurantees the integrity of all trades.

Margin Requirements

When a trader makes an opening trade on an exchange, the exchange may require the trader to deposit with the clearing house some amount of margin, or good faith capital. Such deposits ensure that if the market moves adversely , the trader will still be able to fulfill any future financial obligations resulting from the trade.

In theory, margin deposits at the clearing house still belongs to the individual trader , and as such, any interest or dividends accuring to the margin deposit also belongs to the trader.
